## Three important considerations

**1. The intellectual property provisions are encouraging.**

Mozilla's RFP says copyright, patent, and other proprietary interests in materials produced during the fellowship belong to the Fellow. It also requires code and non-confidential materials to be released under open licenses determined in consultation with Mozilla.

That is much better than assuming the host would own everything you produce. We should still clarify how pre-existing blAIne intellectual property, confidential host information, and jointly developed work would be handled. <Cite refs={["turn767444view0"]}/>

**2. Embedded is designed for the professional stage you're describing.**

Mozilla explicitly identifies rising mid-career builders who have demonstrated technical excellence but haven't achieved field-wide recognition as the people who thrive in Embedded. It also identifies credibility, connections, and host structure as benefits.

That is a particularly important finding: your reasoning for considering Embedded is consistent with Mozilla's own description of the track. <Cite refs={["turn706403view0"]}/>

**3. The fellowship does not automatically solve the office-space requirement.**

The $75,000 stipend is your personal support; the $25,000 is a separate project budget. The RFP describes a remote fellowship with funded travel for key events. We should not assume that the project budget can pay for a dedicated blAIne office or facility without confirming allowable expenses. <Cite refs={["turn767444view0","turn216619search4"]}/>
